# Most Significant Unanswered Question

**Status:** Live analysis as of 2026-08-16
**Scope:** Repository proof surface after GWR closure and universal bounded compression
**Authority:** Derived from `PROOF.md`, `docs/current_headline_results.md`, and `docs/unanswered-questions/chain-horizon-closure/`

## Statement

The most significant unanswered question is: Can `chain_horizon_closure` be turned into a pure PGS next-prime selection rule by deriving the divisor-horizon law from PGS structure, instead of using fallback divisor exhaustion?

This is a proof-architecture and analytic-bridge question. It sits downstream of the closed local theorems and determines how completely the generative engine can be expressed in pure PGS terms.

## Why This Question Matters More Than Other Open Items

The core local theorems are closed on the repository's stated proof surface. `PROOF.md` establishes the direct next-prime rule, the Gap Winner Rule (interior maximizer), and universal bounded compression (including the Prime-Square Proximity Theorem). Residual finite audits and measured surfaces corroborate these results; they do not reopen them. Downstream experimental tracks (RSA endpoint probes, Mersenne generators, DNI-to-zeta reading path) remain open by design and are explicitly tiered as experimental.

The chain-horizon question is different. It is the residual load-bearing gap inside the hierarchical reduced-gap-type engine itself. The engine already has the correct operational shape (search-interval closure, semiprime-shadow seeding, chain-horizon closure, downstream audit). At high scale, however, the terminal decision still falls back to deterministic divisor checking. That fallback constitutes the dominant non-PGS fraction of the high-scale bridge (measured near 56–58 % at the 10^15–10^18 surfaces). Closing it would convert the generator from a hybrid system into a predominantly local, structure-driven selection rule and would raise the conceptual compression and universality of the entire generative result more than any further residual finite audit or experimental probe.

## Evidence That the Question Remains Open

The tracker document `docs/unanswered-questions/chain-horizon-closure/00_question.md` states the question in these exact terms and quantifies the residual non-PGS portion. It identifies the missing object `H(p, s0, chain_state)` and frames the decisive experiment as mining the least-factor maximum of false chain nodes for a PGS-visible bound. Parallel status surfaces (`docs/current_headline_results.md`, `docs/STATUS_TIERS.md`, README) treat GWR and the bounded-compression pillars as closed while leaving the generative high-scale bridge and the broader experimental layer open. No later committed document asserts that the horizon law has been derived from pure PGS quantities. Prior AI answers and solution sketches exist in the same directory; none claim closure.

## What a Successful Answer Would Change

A successful derivation of a tight, PGS-visible bound on the required divisor horizon (or a rigorous demonstration that no substantially smaller bound exists) would eliminate or sharply reduce the high-scale non-PGS remainder. The hierarchical finite-state engine would then operate as a pure structural selection rule rather than a hybrid that still relies on fallback exhaustion at the terminal step. That single improvement would complete the conceptual compression of the generative model, raise its universality claim, and free subsequent work to focus on measured surfaces, RSA-scale probes, and the downstream reading path without a residual architectural dependency inside the core engine.
