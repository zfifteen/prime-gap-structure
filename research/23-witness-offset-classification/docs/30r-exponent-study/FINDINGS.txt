Two chamber positions cannot both have sixteen divisors
2026-09-25

Strongest result
---------------
For p=30r-1 with r odd, the integers p+3 and p+7 are both divisible
by four and differ by four. Exactly one is divisible by four but not
eight. Its divisor count is a multiple of three and therefore is not16.
Consequently tau(p+3)=tau(p+7)=16 is impossible.

The residue of r determines the restricted position:
  r=1 mod4: p+7=4 mod8, so 3 divides tau(p+7).
  r=3 mod4: p+3=4 mod8, so 3 divides tau(p+3).

Proof: 30r+2 and30r+6 are consecutive multiples of4 when r is odd.
One has the form4m with m odd. The divisor-count identity gives
 tau(4m)=3*tau(m).
Reduction modulo8 gives the two branches above. In a matched winning
chamber the restricted count must therefore be at least18, not merely16.
This is an elementary exact deduction under the stated hypothesis. It
is not claimed as a novel theorem, and PROOF.md was not edited.

This is a constraint on possible count profiles, not a replacement rule
for selecting the witness or a prediction of the entire field.

Research assessment
-------------------
The sequence analogy has led to an explicit relationship between
positions: their possible divisor counts are coupled. This relationship
is ordinary exact arithmetic arising from powers of two. It explains
part of the visual pattern, while leaving the joint arrangement needed
for all six neighbors to stay above the threshold unresolved.

The promised decision point has been met in a limited but concrete way:
a constraint beyond the selection inequality was derived and reproduced
on an unexamined band. It is a useful structural explanation, not evidence
by itself of a new deep law or a biological mechanism. The remaining
question is whether richer cross-position constraints explain the three
unaccounted-for original winners and the rarity of complete winning rows.
No replacement candidate was fitted to the fresh band in this experiment.
