Measured result
--------------
Among all 9,376 matched chambers below 10^8, 20 select p+1 and 9,356
select a later integer. Match conditions: p mod 30 = 29, consecutive
prime endpoints p and p+8, and tau(p+1)=16. All twenty winners and their
seven divisor counts match the prior independent audit exactly.

There are 344 losers with exactly one interior count below 16. Every
neighbor position can supply that sole undercut on this measured set:

Position                    p+2  p+3  p+4  p+5  p+6  p+7
Number of sole undercuts    193   19   41   48   41    2

Thus none of the six individual neighbor-threshold checks can be omitted
from the conjunction tau(p+j)>=16, j=2..7, without admitting an actual
losing chamber in this set. This is a concrete nonredundancy result for
these threshold checks, not a claim that no other arithmetic description
could replace them. The conjunction itself is the definition of winning;
its truth is not a newly discovered mechanism.

The final-position candidate
----------------------------
In winners, tau(p+7)>16 in 20/20 cases.
In losers, tau(p+7)>16 in 8400/9356 cases (89.782%);
313 have tau(p+7)=16 and 643 have tau(p+7)<16.

A high final count therefore does not distinguish winning chambers by
itself. It is common among matched losers. Its absence as a tied minimum
in winners remains a same-range observation, not a universal result.
Position p+7 can also be decisive: two actual losers satisfy the other
five neighbor inequalities but fail this final one.

Interpretation
--------------
The sequence analogy now has actual aligned controls: matching most
positions does not ensure the same selection. Every neighboring position
has a demonstrated role in the literal winning condition. The observed
asymmetry of roles, including 193 sole undercuts at p+2 versus 2 at p+7,
is an arithmetic question about these matched chambers. It does not
establish a genetic mechanism or an independent-site probability model.
